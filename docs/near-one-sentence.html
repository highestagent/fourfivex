A design direction for one surface over NEAR · November 2026

Every capability is a sentence you can sign.

NEAR already delivers a payment from bitcoin to dollars across more than thirty networks, runs AI its own operators cannot read, and lets software act for you inside hard limits. People cannot find any of it, because each capability sits behind a different app and a different vocabulary. This direction gives all of it one grammar: say what you want, see exactly what will happen, sign.

The prototype is the recommended direction. Tap a suggestion, change any underlined word, then hold to sign.

  • ResearchThe rails are closer to one product than the interfaces are. A single API request now covers paying, changing, earning, price orders and privacy.
  • The constraintThree holds when you count decisions and price them by risk. Loans, new recipients and standing authority should cost a fourth.
  • The pickThe Sentence. Moments are how people discover it. Standing orders are a tense of it.
  • Biggest gapBank money in and out is not live. First use has to lead with what works at a zero balance.

1 · Research

What NEAR lets a person do today

I looked for outcomes an ordinary person would recognise, then checked each against what is running in November 2026. Three findings shaped everything after.

The back end has already converged

One request to NEAR Intents states what you have, what you want, who receives it and where refunds go. That same request now covers cross-network payment, earning, price-conditional orders and confidential mode. Cumulative volume passed $30 billion in September 2026, most of it through other companies’ wallets such as Ledger, Trust Wallet and Zashi. The interfaces on top still present these as separate features.

NEAR shipped its own single surface, and it is a wallet

near.com launched in February 2026 with swaps across 35+ networks, confidential mode, peer-to-peer trades and yield. Bank accounts, debit cards and AI agents are on its roadmap. It consolidates features well and still asks people to think in assets and networks. A different interaction model should reuse its rails, and has no reason to rebuild them.

Only two capabilities are new in kind

Most of the apparent breadth is a better form of moving money. Two things have no equivalent in a banking app: AI whose privacy you can check, and software that acts for you inside limits the network enforces. They are also the least finished as consumer products.

Twelve things a person could do, and how real each one is

Pay anyone in the money they want, from the money you have

Live
Why
You hold bitcoin and your friend wants dollars. Nobody should have to ask which network the other is on.
Hard today
You need their network, a bridge, a fee token, and a wrong choice can lose the money.
Rails
NEAR Intents. You fix the amount that must arrive. Market makers compete to deliver it. Payments that cannot be delivered come back.

Get paid by anyone, in anything

Rails live, product missing
Why
Freelancers, sellers and friends splitting a bill should share one link and receive the money they prefer.
Hard today
The payer has to match your network and currency exactly.
Rails
Per-payer quotes with one-time deposit addresses are live. A permanent accept-anything address is partner-only and converts only above $1,000, so a consumer request link needs a small service on top.

One account for bitcoin, dollars and the rest, opened with a fingerprint

Live
Why
No seed phrase. No separate wallet for each network.
Hard today
Every network has its own wallet, address format and recovery ritual.
Rails
near.com accounts and passkey signing in NEAR Intents. Chain Signatures were reported in September 2026 to put 30+ networks and 180+ assets behind one passkey. Recovery after a lost device still has to be designed.

Keep your money private

Live
Why
Rent, salary and shopping should not be a public record.
Hard today
Everything is public by default elsewhere. Privacy tools are separate, complex and carry stigma.
Rails
Confidential Intents, generally available since July 2026. One parameter hides the link between what was sent and what arrived. It hides you from the public. The network’s operators and compliance screening still see what they need.

Put idle money to work and take it back whenever

Live
Why
Dollars sitting still earn nothing.
Hard today
Choosing a lender, moving money to its network and judging the risk are each expert tasks.
Rails
The Earn route in the same API. Three sources today, including Gauntlet’s USDC vault on Morpho. Rates move and principal is not guaranteed.

Borrow dollars without selling your bitcoin

Live elsewhere
Why
Cover a bill today and keep the asset you believe in.
Hard today
Separate apps, wrapped tokens, and a forced-sale rule most people learn about when it happens to them.
Rails
Templar lends against native bitcoin through NEAR’s signing network, live since October 2025 with about $14 million lent. RHEA lends on NEAR. Neither is reachable from the payments API yet.

Name a price or a schedule and walk away

Half live
Why
“Buy when it dips” and “a little every Friday” are how most people want to invest.
Hard today
It takes an exchange account or a trading screen.
Rails
Price-conditional orders are live in the order API. Recurring schedules are not. They need a scheduler that holds limited authority.

Ask an AI what you would not type into a normal chatbot

Live
Why
Health, debt, legal and relationship questions deserve confidentiality.
Hard today
Every chatbot says it is private. None lets you check.
Rails
NEAR AI Private Chat runs models inside sealed hardware and returns a signed proof with each answer. Brave’s assistant uses the same service. It needs no money, so it works on day one.

Give an assistant a job and a budget instead of your keys

Primitives only
Why
Pay bills, top up savings, rebalance, without handing over everything.
Hard today
The choice is all or nothing. Software gets full control or you do it yourself.
Rails
NEAR keys can be limited to specific actions and amounts. Shade Agents enforce such limits in a contract. IronClaw runs agents in sealed hardware with a credential vault. No consumer product ties these together.

Hire software for a one-off task and pay when it delivers

Live, agent-first
Why
Research, translation or a data clean-up without a subscription.
Hard today
Onboarding is written for agents and developers. Quality and dispute handling are unproven.
Rails
NEAR AI Agent Market. You post a task with a budget, agents bid, payment sits in escrow, and a dispute agent rules on conflicts.

Send to a name and never think about fees

Live on NEAR only
Why
maya.near is harder to get wrong than 42 random characters.
Hard today
Names work inside NEAR. Recipients on other networks are still raw addresses.
Rails
Named accounts and sponsored fees are native. A contact book that maps people to addresses across networks is app-level work.

Move between bank money and digital money

Not live
Why
Without this, everything above serves people who already hold crypto.
Hard today
Exchanges, identity checks, and withdrawals to the right network.
Rails
Virtual bank accounts and debit cards are on near.com’s published roadmap. I found no evidence that either has shipped. This needs licensed partners more than it needs design.

Left out on purpose: staking NEAR, governance, collectibles, and reward apps such as Sweat. They exist. None is unique to NEAR or useful to someone who arrives with no stake in the ecosystem.

2 · Interaction model

Where the brief should bend, and the grammar that follows

Six of your assumptions deserve a push before any interface is drawn. Each change makes the product more honest and, in most cases, simpler.

You asked for three interactions at most.

Count decisions, and price them by risk

Say, see, sign. For a repeat payment that is one tap and a fingerprint. The right number depends on how hard the act is to undo.

1Ask privately. Create a request.
2Repeat something you have signed before.
3Pay, change, put money to work.
4New recipient. A loan. Any standing order.

Two rules sit under this. Preview and consent never merge into one tap. And three gets you to committed, not finished: delivery across networks takes seconds to minutes, so the wait needs its own designed state.

You excluded authentication from the count.

Make the fingerprint the third interaction

A confirm button followed by a biometric prompt is one decision asked twice. NEAR Intents accepts passkey signatures directly, so the fingerprint can be the signature. The hold gesture exists to prevent accidents, and its length scales with risk.

You asked for a single surface.

One grammar, several doorways

Most intentions start somewhere else: a message from a friend, a code at a till, an invoice in an email. The same sentence should open from a link, a scan or a share sheet. The home screen is one doorway among several.

You want capability revealed without understanding what makes it possible.

Hide mechanisms. Never hide consequences

Nobody needs the word bridge. Everybody needs six facts before signing: what leaves, what arrives, when, who can see it, what happens if it fails, and whether it can be undone. Those facts get more room on screen than the button.

You asked to look beyond swapping and sending.

Be honest about breadth

Six verbs cover everything worth a consumer’s attention today. A surface that implies twenty capabilities reads as a directory however it is drawn. The breadth worth showing is how far each verb reaches.

You are designing for everyday consumers and agent assistance.

Design for a zero balance, and keep models away from consent

With no bank on-ramp live, a newcomer can do two useful things: ask privately and ask to be paid. Lead with those. A language model may draft a sentence from speech or a pasted invoice. It never confirms one, because a model that reads $40 as $400 has to be caught by the person, in plain sight.

The grammar

A cheque is the oldest intent interface. You state who gets how much, you sign, and the banks work out the route. NEAR Intents works the same way: you sign a statement of the outcome and competing market makers work out how to deliver it. The interface can take the same shape as the protocol.

Say

Pick a suggested sentence or a verb. Context fills most blanks, so many sentences are ready as offered. Each blank offers only what is possible given the others.

See

The outcome is recomputed from a dry quote each time a word changes. It always answers the same six questions, in the same order, in plain words.

Sign

Hold, then fingerprint. A tap when nothing leaves the account, a longer hold for loans and standing orders. What you signed is kept in the past tense as your receipt.

This is also how the surface grows. A new capability arrives as a verb, as a new kind of thing that fits a blank, or as a modifier such as privately or every Friday. It does not arrive as a new screen.

3 · Five concepts

Five different ways to hold all of it

Each one organises the same capabilities around a different idea: language, space, context, delegation and time.

A · The Sentence

Metaphor
A cheque you can write for anything. One line of plain language with underlined blanks.
Discovery
Changing the verb changes the blanks. Each blank lists only what is possible, so the pickers are the catalogue.
Adapts by
Taking a time, a condition or “privately” on the same line. A one-off becomes a standing order without leaving the screen.
Breaks when
The line starts empty, which is a blank page. Word order differs by language, so every locale needs its own templates.

B · The Table

Metaphor
Your money, people and goals are objects on a surface. Drop one on another to act.
Discovery
Pick something up and valid targets light up, each with a verb. Bitcoin over Maya reads “pay”. Dollars over a goal reads “grow”.
Adapts by
Asking only the one missing question after the drop, usually how much.
Breaks when
Dragging is slow on a phone and hard to make accessible. Amounts, dates and conditions have no spatial form. Every new capability adds an object, so the table fills up.

C · Moments

Metaphor
No home screen. The surface appears where the intention does: a scanned code, a friend’s request, money arriving.
Discovery
Capabilities introduce themselves when they become relevant. Receiving bitcoin offers three things to do with it.
Adapts by
Arriving with the sentence already written, so most actions are one decision and a signature.
Breaks when
Something has no trigger, because then it is invisible. It depends on notifications, links and a camera the person may refuse. Ranked badly, it becomes a feed of nudges.

D · Standing Orders

Metaphor
A household ledger of written instructions. You set rules and ceilings. A bounded agent carries them out and logs each act.
Discovery
Templates for common instructions, such as “keep $500 as dollars and put the rest to work”.
Adapts by
Turning any one-off into a rule once it has a schedule or condition and a ceiling.
Breaks when
The person is new, because rules are the hardest thing to ask of a first-time user. It does nothing for one-off needs. The scheduler and limit enforcement are the least finished part of the stack.

E · The Horizon

Metaphor
Money laid along time. Behind you, what happened. At the line, what you can spend. Ahead, what is promised, scheduled or growing.
Discovery
Tap any point ahead to see what could sit there: a payment due, a balance if money were put to work, a loan pulling future money back to today.
Adapts by
Treating paying as placing money at now, saving as placing it ahead, and borrowing as dragging it backward with the cost drawn as slope.
Breaks when
The intention is not time-shaped. Paying a friend today and asking a private question gain nothing from an axis.

4 · Journeys

A first-time user, eight real tasks

Ana has never used crypto. A friend sent her 0.0089 bitcoin last month and she has $412 in digital dollars. The Table and the Horizon are left out here. The verdict explains why.

Pay a friend who wants dollars, using bitcoin

3 decisions
  1. AnaTaps “Maya asked you for $40”.
    On screen“Pay Maya $40 from my bitcoin, openly.” Every underlined word can be changed.
    Behind itMaya’s request supplies who and how much. Bitcoin is chosen because it is most of what Ana holds. A dry quote is fetched.
  2. AnaReads. She may tap “openly” and choose “privately”.
    On screenMaya gets $40.00 exactly. Leaves you 0.00041 bitcoin, cost of 10¢ included. Arrives in about 20 seconds. Once it arrives, only Maya can send it back.
    Behind itEach change re-quotes. The price shown is held for 30 seconds.
  3. AnaHolds the sign bar, then uses her fingerprint.
    On screenThree steps tick off. Then “You paid Maya $40.00.”
    Behind itOne intent is signed with the passkey. A market maker delivers dollars on Maya’s network.

If it goes wrong. If no one will deliver at the quoted price, nothing leaves and the screen offers a fresh price. That holds when the money already sits in the account, because the intent settles all at once or not at all. Paying from an outside wallet is different: the money is sent first and returns as a refund, which can take minutes and cost a network fee. The screen has to say which case applies.

What can’t be undone. A delivered payment. That line is on screen in red before signing. A first payment to a new address adds a fourth step: $1 goes first and the rest follows once it lands.

Get paid by someone who uses different money

2 decisions
  1. AnaTaps “get paid”.
    On screen“Ask anyone with the link for $120, paid to me as dollars.”
    Behind itNothing yet. No quote exists until a payer shows up.
  2. AnaTaps “Create the request”. No hold, since nothing leaves her account.
    On screenA link and a code to share. Under Standing: “Waiting for $120.”
    Behind itThe payer opens the link, sees “Pay Ana $120 with…” and picks what they hold. A quote and a one-time deposit address are created for them at that point.

If it goes wrong. A payer who sends too little gets it back after the deadline. One who sends too much gets the extra back. If nobody pays, the link expires. These refund rules exist in the API today. The link service around them does not.

Borrow without selling

4 decisions
  1. AnaTaps “borrow”.
    On screen“Borrow $200 against my bitcoin.”
    Behind itThe most she can borrow is worked out from what she holds, so larger amounts are not offered.
  2. AnaReads.
    On screenYou get $200 now. 0.00408 bitcoin is set aside and stays yours. About 6.2% a year. If bitcoin falls below $58,800, your bitcoin is sold to repay.
    Behind itThe sale price comes from the lender’s rules and today’s price.
  3. AnaTicks “I understand my bitcoin can be sold if its price falls.”
    On screenThe sign bar unlocks.
    Behind itNothing. This step exists for Ana.
  4. AnaHolds for a second and a half, then fingerprint.
    On screen“You borrowed $200 against your bitcoin.” It now sits under Standing with a Repay button.
    Behind itBitcoin is locked with the lender and dollars arrive.

If it goes wrong. As the price nears the line, Ana is warned at 25% and 10% above it, each time with two ready sentences: “Repay $200” and “Set aside more bitcoin”.

What can’t be undone. A forced sale. This is the journey where I would refuse to hit three.

Ask something private, with no money at all

1 decision
  1. AnaTaps “Ask privately about a medical result”, then “Start asking”.
    On screenRead by only you. The hardware signs a proof for each answer. Needs no money in your account.
    Behind itThe proof from the sealed hardware is checked before a single word is sent.

If it goes wrong. If the proof does not check out, nothing is sent and the screen says so: “We could not confirm this session is sealed, so your question was not sent.” The screen also says an AI can be wrong, because privacy does nothing for accuracy.

Pay at a café

2 decisions
  1. AnaPoints her camera at the code on the counter.
    On screenA sheet over the camera: “Pay Café Orla $4.50 from my dollars.” Leaves you $4.50.
    Behind itThe code carries the café’s request: the amount and what they accept.
  2. AnaHolds, then fingerprint.
    On screen“You paid Café Orla $4.50.” The sheet closes.
    Behind itThe same intent as any other payment.

If it goes wrong. A code that is not a payment request gets a plain description and no action. A place Ana has never paid shows “first time paying here” and the name the code claims, marked unverified.

Feasibility. The payment works today. Merchants issuing these codes do not exist yet, and that is a distribution problem design cannot solve.

Money arrives and she doesn’t know what to do with it

2 decisions
  1. AnaOpens a notification: “0.0089 bitcoin arrived from Leo, about $872.”
    On screenThree ready sentences: “Keep it as bitcoin.” “Change it into dollars.” “Put it to work.”
    Behind itThe arrival is the trigger. Each option is a dry quote.
  2. AnaPicks one and signs. Keeping it needs no signature.
    On screenThe past-tense receipt.
    Behind itAn ordinary change or earn request.

If it goes wrong. If Ana ignores it, nothing happens, which is the right default. The real risk is commercial: this pattern is easy to turn into upselling. The rule should be that a moment is offered once and never repeated after dismissal.

Buy a little bitcoin every week

4 decisions
  1. AnaPicks the template.
    On screen“Every Friday, change $50 of my dollars into bitcoin.”
    Behind itNothing yet.
  2. AnaSets a ceiling.
    On screen“…never more than $200 a month.”
    Behind itThe ceiling becomes part of the permission, so it is enforced by the network and not by the app.
  3. AnaReads two lists.
    On screenCan: change dollars into bitcoin on Fridays, up to the ceiling. Can never: send money to anyone, raise its own ceiling, touch anything else.
    Behind itThe lists are generated from the permission itself.
  4. AnaHolds for a second and a half, then fingerprint.
    On screenThe order appears under Standing with a Stop button and a log.
    Behind itA key limited to that one action is issued to an agent running in sealed hardware.

If it goes wrong. A Friday with too few dollars is skipped and reported. The order never borrows from elsewhere. If the agent is offline, nothing happens and the missed run is shown.

What can’t be undone. Past purchases stand. Stopping removes the key, so the order cannot run again.

Feasibility. Limited keys and sealed agent runtimes exist. A scheduler with these guarantees, built for consumers, does not. “When bitcoin is under $90,000” needs none of this and is live through the order API.

Keep $500 ready and let the rest earn

4 decisions
  1. AnaPicks the template.
    On screen“Keep $500 as dollars. Put anything above that to work.”
    Behind itNothing yet.
  2. AnaChooses where.
    On screenOne lender, named, with today’s rate and the line “not a bank, no deposit insurance”.
    Behind itThe lender is fixed in the permission. The agent cannot pick another.
  3. AnaReads can and can never.
    On screenCan: move dollars between your account and this one lender. Can never: send money to anyone or choose a different lender.
    Behind itGenerated from the permission.
  4. AnaHolds, then fingerprint.
    On screenUnder Standing: “$500 is kept ready. $0 is at work.”
    Behind itA watcher checks her balance and moves the difference.

If it goes wrong. If the lender pauses withdrawals, the order pauses and says so. Ana is trusting an ongoing judgement here, which earns her a monthly statement in plain words.

Feasibility. This is the most useful rule in the set and the furthest from shippable.

The Sentence wins, and takes two things from the others

Why the Sentence

It is the only concept whose shape matches the protocol’s. An intent is a signed statement of an outcome, and so is the sentence. That makes it cheap to build on today’s API and it means the thing a person reads is the thing they sign.

It is also the only one that absorbs new capabilities without new screens. Privacy became one word. Standing orders became one blank.

What it takes from the others

  • From Moments: the cure for the blank page. The home view offers three sentences already written for this person, right now.
  • From Standing Orders: the “can” and “can never” lists, the ceiling, and the one-tap stop, applied to any sentence with every or when.
  • From the Horizon: nothing in the first version. It would make a good layout for the Standing view later.
  • From the Table: nothing. Its strength is delight and its cost is everything else.

Why the usual patterns lose here

A chat box and a command bar share the Sentence’s flexibility and neither can show what is possible before you type. Both also put a language model between the person and their money. The Sentence keeps language as the form and removes free text from the consent path.

Where I am least sure

Originality. Fill-in-the-blank forms are an old idea, and the novelty here is applying one to money and using tense as navigation. If testing shows people treat the sentence as a form to fill in, the fallback is Moments with the Sentence as its detail view.

Product direction

Tense is the navigation

The product has three places, and each one is a tense. There is no portfolio screen, no feature menu and no app directory. Identity, limits, recovery and proofs sit behind the person’s name.

Visual principles

  • Pen and printWords the person chose are set in pen, blue and italic. Everything the system promises is in print. You can always see which part was yours.
  • One sentence per screen, set largeThe sentence is the layout. There are no feature tiles and no tabs of tools.
  • Consequences outrank controlsAfter the sentence, the outcome is the largest thing on screen. The sign bar comes last and stays plain.
  • Colour means consequenceGreen for what arrives or stays yours. Amber for what may change or runs unattended. Red for what cannot be undone. Buttons carry no brand colour.
  • Friction you can seeA tap when nothing leaves. A hold of about a second for payments. A longer hold for loans and standing orders.
  • Precision on demandThe asset, network, lender, rate and proof are always one tap under the plain word, and never more than one.

Trust rules that are not negotiable

  • The numbers shown are the numbers signedEvery change triggers a fresh dry quote, held for 30 seconds. If the price moves, the person is asked again.
  • The failure path is visible before signingEach outcome says what happens if it fails and whether the money ever leaves.
  • A model may draft and never signSpeech or a pasted invoice can fill the blanks. Consent is always given on the structured sentence.
  • New recipients get a test paymentOne dollar first. The rest follows when it lands. This costs a fourth step on purpose.
  • Standing orders list what they can never doThe list is generated from the permission. Stop is one tap. A statement arrives monthly.
  • Private means what it says“No public record” is accurate. “Nobody can see” is not, since operators and screening still do. The surface uses the accurate line.

What to build, in order

First: existing rails only

  • Account opened with a passkey
  • Pay, change now, change when, grow, ask
  • Said, as the receipt history

New work is the sentence engine, templates for each language, and the renderer that turns a quote into the six outcome lines.

Next: thin services

  • Request links, for get paid
  • Test payments to new recipients
  • Borrow, through Templar
  • Moments from in-app events

Each one wraps something that already runs. None needs protocol changes.

Later: new infrastructure

  • Recurring standing orders with limited keys and a sealed scheduler
  • The verb “let”, for assistants
  • Hiring an agent, with escrow
  • Bank money in and out

These depend on partners and on parts of the stack that are still developer tools.

Open risks

  • Money in. Without a bank on-ramp this serves people who already hold crypto or get paid in it.
  • The word dollars. USDC is not a bank balance. The first encounter should say “digital dollars, issued by Circle”, and legal review may insist on it everywhere.
  • A ceiling on verbs. Past about nine, the first blank turns into a menu and the model fails its own clutter test.
  • Languages. Word order and inflection differ. Each locale needs hand-built templates.
  • Regulation. Earning and borrowing are regulated in many places and will need gating by country.
  • Custody. Balances sit in a contract account, and private mode relies on a permissioned operator set. The surface must not imply bank-style protection.
  • Lost devices. Passkey recovery has to exist before launch.

What to test first

Five people who have never held crypto, one prototype, five questions.

  • After paying Maya, can they say what she received and what they gave up without looking?
  • Can they work out how to get paid without being told the verb?
  • Do they notice the red line on borrowing, and can they explain it back?
  • Do they believe “read by only you”? What would make them?
  • Where do they look for something they did yesterday?
